Blocksmith Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 24, 2026

Blocksmith is a mixed-reality building game for Meta Quest. This page explains what the game does with information about you. In short: it sends nothing to us. If you play co-op, what you build and how you move are shared with the other players in your game, and online games pass through Unity's relay servers.

What stays on your headset

  • Your builds and progress (saves, coins, unlocks, settings) are stored only on your headset.
  • Photos and exported builds. Photos you take with the in-game camera are saved to your headset's Pictures folder, and builds you export to its Download folder. They stay there unless you move them.
  • Your room. To place blocks on your real table and floor, the game uses passthrough and the room layout (surfaces) that your headset provides. This happens on the device. Blocksmith does not record, store or send camera images or a scan of your room. (In co-op, the size of the tables your builds stand on is shared with the other players as plain boxes; see below.)
  • Hand and controller tracking is used while you play and is not stored. (In co-op, the other players see where your head and hands are while the game lasts; see below.)

What we collect

Nothing. Blocksmith has no accounts of its own, no ads, no analytics, no chat and no voice chat, and we run no servers, so nothing about you reaches us while you play. If you report a player or write to us, we receive what you send (see "Reporting a player").

Playing together (co-op)

Co-op is optional. Nothing below happens unless you host a game or join one. Up to four players can share one workshop, either on the same local network (Wi-Fi) or online.

What the other players in your game receive:

  • your builds: their shapes, colours, finishes and mechanisms, and the changes you make to them;
  • where your head and hands are, the tool in your hand and where you point, so they can see you as a simple avatar while the game lasts;
  • the player name you pick from a list in the game and a colour. It is never your Meta account name, and you cannot type a name;
  • the height and size of the tables your builds stand on, as plain boxes, so the builds have something to stand on for them;
  • whether your game allows online play (a yes or no), so that the host of an online game can keep it to players who may play online.

What is never shared: camera images, the layout or scan of your room, your voice, typed text, your Meta account name, ID or friends.

The host's game keeps what everyone built there in the host's own save. A guest keeps nothing from the host's game: when you leave, your own workshop comes back as it was.

Co-op on a local network

On the same network, the headsets (or PCs) talk to each other directly. A hosted game announces itself on that network with the host's picked name and the number of players, so that others on the same network can find it. None of this passes through us or Unity.

Two Quests in one room. To show the same builds in the same place for both players, the host's headset shares a spatial anchor on the floor using Meta's shared spatial anchors. This needs your headset's Enhanced Spatial Services setting to be on, and the anchor data is stored and handled by Meta under Meta's privacy policy. We never receive it. Turning Enhanced Spatial Services off deletes spatial data you shared from Meta's servers.

Online co-op (Unity Relay)

Online play is off until you switch it on in the game's Co-op menu. To connect players on different networks, the game uses Unity Gaming Services from Unity Technologies, our service provider:

  • An anonymous player ID. When you first host or join online, Unity Authentication creates a random player ID. It is not linked to your Meta account, your name or your email. The game keeps it on your headset to sign in again next time.
  • Relay servers. Game traffic between players goes through Unity's relay servers, encrypted on the way. The relay servers see your internet (IP) address and the anonymous player ID. A six-letter join code, which the host gives to the people they want to play with, only works while the host's game is open.

Unity keeps the IP address and player ID that Relay uses for 30 days, and keeps the anonymous player ID until it is deleted (see "Deleting your data"). Unity processes this data only on our behalf, under its data processing terms. Unity is based in the United States, and its relay servers are in the United States, Europe, South America, Asia and Australia (the one nearest the players is used). We do not receive your IP address. See also Unity's privacy policy.

How co-op data is used

Only to run the game you asked for: to connect the players (network communication) and to sign in to the relay service (authentication). The IP address and the anonymous player ID are never used to contact you, to build a profile of you, for advertising or for anything else, and nobody sells them. The game does not send them anywhere but Unity's sign-in and relay services, and we do not ask Unity for them.

Reporting a player

If someone in a co-op game behaves badly, press the Meta button and choose Report. The host can also remove a player from the game, and anyone can leave at any time. Reports are collected by Meta's reporting service and emailed to us. A report holds what you write in it and, if you choose to give it, your email address. We use reports only to look into what happened and to make the game safer, and delete them within one year.

Children

Blocksmith is made for players aged 10 and up, including Meta preteen accounts (ages 10 to 12). It collects no personal information from anyone: no accounts, no chat, no voice, no ads and no purchases inside the game. The coins in the game's shop are only earned by playing and can never be bought.

Online play is not available on preteen accounts. When the game starts, it asks the Meta Horizon platform which age group your account is in (child, teen or adult), as Meta requires of apps for mixed ages. If the account is a preteen account, or the age group can't be checked, the game switches online play off. Preteens can still play co-op with people on the same local network, where nothing passes through Unity or us. The age group itself stays on the headset and is not sent to us or stored; the host of a game you join is only told whether online play is allowed.

No player can type a name or a message, and no voice is sent. Parents can approve or block apps from Meta's parent controls (Meta Family Center), and anyone can report a player through the Meta button.

Deleting your data

  • Uninstalling Blocksmith removes everything it saved on your headset (photos and builds you exported stay in your Pictures and Download folders until you delete them).
  • The anonymous online player ID: press "Forget my online ID" in the game's Co-op menu. It deletes the ID at Unity and on your headset. If you uninstalled first, write to us and we will ask Unity to delete what it holds; the relay's records expire after 30 days anyway.
  • Reports you sent us: write to us and we will delete them.

Meta

When the game starts, it asks the Meta Horizon platform whether your account owns Blocksmith (this is required for apps sold on the Meta Horizon Store). Meta handles that check under its own privacy policy: meta.com/legal/quest/privacy-policy.

Changes

If Blocksmith starts handling information differently, this page will say so first, with a new date at the top.
